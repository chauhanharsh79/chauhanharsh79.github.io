Security

Who is on site, where they are, and whether they should be there.
Intruder / Unknown Person Detection +
Detects
A person whose face is not on your enrolled list of family, staff or contractors.
Where it helps
Perimeter, entrances, driveways, yards, store rooms and staff-only floors.
Example
Someone climbs the boundary wall at 2:50 AM and walks toward the house.
Configurable response
Mobile alert with snapshot and clip · voice warning · floodlight · siren where installed.
Known-Person Recognition +
Detects
Enrolled family members, staff and contractors, so routine movement does not raise alarms.
Where it helps
Main gate, reception, lobby and entry cameras.
Example
Your housekeeper arrives at 9 AM and is recognised instead of flagged as an intruder.
Configurable response
Silent log entry · named notification where you want one.
Unauthorised Access +
Detects
A known person in a place or at a time they are not permitted.
Where it helps
Cash offices, server and records rooms, pharmacy and inventory stores.
Example
An enrolled employee enters the stores at 11 PM, outside their permitted hours.
Configurable response
Named mobile alert with camera and clip · evidence retained.
Restricted-Zone Monitoring +
Detects
Any person inside a marked area during the hours it is off limits.
Where it helps
Plant rooms, racks, cabinets, dispatch bays and any area drawn as a zone.
Example
A person stands at the stock racks after the permitted window has closed.

Local voice warning in that area · light · silent alert and clip to the desk.
Banned Person / Watchlist +
Detects

Main gate, reception and visitor entrances.
Example

Alert with the matched photo so the desk confirms before acting.

Escalating alert · voice warning · evidence clip.

Every camera on the system, continuously.

Restricted Zones & Access

A camera records the room. A zone watches it.

Your store rooms, server or records room, plant rooms and entrances are already on camera. What is missing is a rule: which area, during which hours, and who is allowed in it. Parvekshak marks each one as a zone and alerts the security desk the moment it is broken — not three weeks later when someone reviews the footage.

Rule part 01 Area

A polygon drawn on the camera view — the racks or the cabinet, not the whole frame.

+
Rule part 02 Permitted hours

Working hours, night only, or always.

+
Rule part 03 Who is allowed

Enrolled staff only, a named list, or nobody after hours.

=
Result Named alert

Security desk, in seconds, with the clip — and the area can answer back with voice and light.

Speaker / Voice Warning

A local warning in the area that was breached — challenging the person where they are, not across the whole site.

Siren / Hooter

Audible escalation where the site permits it, linked to confirmed events rather than every movement.

Floodlight / Light

The area lights up as configured, deterring the person and giving the camera a usable image.

Mobile Alert

A notification to the security desk, owner or management with the frame that triggered it.

Evidence Clip

The video around the event, kept on the appliance for verification and follow-up.

Reports

Compliance, attendance, counting and operational records available from the console.

Response devices are installed and configured per site. Sirens and floodlights are used only where the premises permits them.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are all of these available on every deployment?

No, and that is deliberate. Capabilities are selected per site during the survey and configured at commissioning — a home runs a handful, a plant may run security, safety and operations together. Some capabilities need particular camera placement, lighting or lens choices to work well, which is part of what the survey establishes.

Can we add capabilities later?

Yes. Once the cameras and the appliance are in, additional capabilities are a configuration change rather than new hardware on the wall — subject to the cameras covering the right areas.

How accurate is the detection?

Accuracy depends on the camera, the lighting, the angle and the scene. That is why deployments start with a survey and end with commissioning: coverage, zones and thresholds are tuned on your site rather than assumed. We would rather set expectations correctly than overclaim.

Does face recognition mean my data goes somewhere?

No. Enrolled faces, clips and reports are held on the appliance inside your premises, and users and roles control who can see what.

What triggers a physical response rather than just an alert?

You decide, per zone. A common configuration is a local voice warning and light in the breached area with a silent alert and clip to the desk, keeping sirens and floodlights for the perimeter and only where the site permits them.

Which of these does your site need?

Book a free survey. We walk the premises, mark the zones with your team and recommend the capabilities that are worth enabling — and the ones that are not.
